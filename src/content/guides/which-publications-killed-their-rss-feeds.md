RSS did not die; it went unadvertised. Most major publishers still emit feeds, but a significant minority have retired them, restricted them, or let them rot, and no announcement accompanies any of it. What follows is a maintained reference, drawn from Inkline's July 2026 verification pass, of where the major titles stand. It will be wrong within a quarter, which is why it carries a date and gets re-checked with the registry.

## Which major publications have retired public RSS?

Inkline's July 2026 verification found the pattern is retreat by neglect rather than announcement. The clearest cases among major English-language titles:

| Publication | Public feed status | What still works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Times / The Sunday Times | Retired public feeds | Resolved inside Inkline via verified alternatives |
| Reuters | Public feeds stopped updating after the site moved to registration | Desk coverage resolved via verified alternatives |
| Bloomberg | No public RSS provision | Monitored via verified alternatives and syndication |
| Financial Times | Feeds available; several desk feeds no longer publicly listed | 12 sections verified, including three unlisted |
| The Guardian | Extensive public feeds, well maintained | Nearly every desk syndicated |
| BBC News | Extensive public feeds | Desk feeds trail live pages by minutes during breaking news |

## Why do publishers retire feeds?

Three reasons recur. Feeds bypass the page, and the page is where subscriptions are sold and advertising is measured, so a publisher moving to a hard paywall has little incentive to keep syndicating. Feeds are also unowned internally: they break in redesigns and nobody's job is to notice. And some publishers replaced them with newsletters, which give the publisher the relationship instead of giving it to the reader.

The result for anyone doing professional monitoring is a slow leak. A media list built in 2022 has quietly lost sources every year since, and the failure mode is silence rather than error: the feed simply stops, and the monitor stops seeing a publication without ever being told.

## What to do about it

Assume decay and schedule against it. A monitoring list is infrastructure, and infrastructure gets inspected: every source you rely on should be checked at least quarterly for whether it still updates, still covers the desks you think it covers, and still resolves at the same place.

Prefer sources of record for the check itself. A publication's own site is the only authority on whether its feeds exist; third-party directories are overwhelmingly stale, and repeating their listings is how dead feeds circulate for years.

Where a feed is genuinely gone, decide per title whether the publication matters enough to resolve another way. For the titles above marked as resolved, Inkline maintains verified alternatives inside the app, checked on the same quarterly pass as everything else. That is hand labour, which is the honest reason it is the product rather than a blog post.
