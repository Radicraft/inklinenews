UK business coverage still travels by feed, and most of the titles that matter maintain them. The short answer: the Financial Times, City A.M., The Guardian's business desk, BBC News business, Reuters UK and The Economist all had working provision at the registry pass of 17 July 2026. What differs is how much each one gives away before the paywall, and how quickly.

The FT runs section feeds across its desks and paywalls the articles themselves; headlines and standfirsts arrive in full, which is enough for monitoring and a decision to click. Details on sections and cadence are on the [Financial Times page](/publications/financial-times/).

City A.M. remains free to read and quick to publish, with markets coverage that lands before most morning meetings. See the [City A.M. page](/publications/city-a-m/).

The Guardian's business desk publishes one of the most complete free feed sets of any UK national. The [Guardian page](/publications/the-guardian/) lists all eighteen sections we verify.

The BBC's business coverage arrives on its own feed among the corporation's extensive set, free and fast. The [BBC News page](/publications/bbc-news/) has the full list.

Reuters stopped public feed provision, a change most directories still have not caught. Inkline resolves Reuters coverage inside the app instead; the [Reuters page](/publications/reuters/) explains what that means in practice.

The Economist runs section feeds with paywalled articles and a weekly cadence that suits digest reading rather than monitoring. See the [Economist page](/publications/the-economist/).

For trade coverage of the communications industry itself, [PR Week](/publications/pr-week/) and [Press Gazette](/publications/press-gazette/) both maintain working feeds; PR Week sits behind a paywall and Press Gazette meters.

Every date above is real: each publication page states when a person last opened and checked its provision. When a publisher retires a feed, the page says so and the directory catches it on the next pass.
