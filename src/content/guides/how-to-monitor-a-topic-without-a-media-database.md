Enterprise media databases earn their fees on scale: tens of thousands of sources, sentiment scoring, coverage reports. A comms team of one rarely needs that. What you need is to see every mention of your clients, your sector and your competitors, early enough to act, without reading everything.

That takes three layers.

First, the publications that matter to you, followed directly. A verified feed from a publication delivers everything it publishes, the moment it publishes, with no algorithm in between. The hard part has always been knowing which feeds exist and which quietly died; that is the problem Inkline's registry of 817 hand-picked, feed-verified publications exists to solve, and the [directory is browsable here](/publications/).

Second, topic monitoring for everything outside your list. A client can be named by a title you have never heard of, in a country you do not cover. Monitoring a topic across the whole web, with an alert when new coverage appears, catches those. In Inkline you type the topic once and it runs alongside your feeds.

Third, a brief. Following fifty publications and five topics produces more than anyone reads before nine. A morning brief that names what is notable, and stays quiet when nothing is, turns the pile into a decision. Inkline writes that brief on your phone with Apple Intelligence, which is also why your monitor list never appears in a vendor's analytics.

What this setup will not do: build coverage reports, score sentiment, or export to a client deck automatically. If you need those weekly, you need the enterprise contract too. Most practitioners need them quarterly and need the watching daily. The watching is the part you can now run yourself, for the price of a paperback a year.
